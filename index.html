Privacy Policy

Chai Pe Charcha · Last updated October 10, 2026

Privacy · Terms

The short version. Chai Pe Charcha has no accounts, no ads and no tracking. What you and your family or neighbors share lives in iCloud, not on our servers, and we can't read it. You can leave a group, or delete one you created, at any time.

Who we are

Chai Pe Charcha is an iPhone app made by Tejash, an independent developer. In this policy, "we" means the developer. You can reach us at tejash.apps@gmail.com.

What the app stores, and where

WhatWhere it lives
Your family name and your neighbor name (the names others see next to your votes, messages, posts and replies)On your phone, and in the iCloud space of each parivar or mohalla you join
Parivar content: the family question and votes, events, RSVPs, who is bringing what, event chat messages, reportsIn iCloud, in a space owned by the person who created the parivar and shared with its members

The app also uses an opaque identifier that iCloud gives your Apple ID for this app. It is not your name or email. The app uses it to tell which posts and votes are yours. We never see your Apple ID, email address or phone number.

What we can and can't see

  • We can't see what is inside a parivar or mohalla. It is stored in iCloud, in the account of the person who created the group, and the app has no server of ours in between.
  • We can see invite code records in the public database: the code, the group's name, the invite link and the dates. These records have no names, posts or messages in them.
  • We don't run analytics, advertising or tracking tools, and the app contains no third-party SDKs.

How the app uses your information

  • To show you your parivar, your mohalla and your events, and to let the people in a group see what is shared with them.
  • To send you notifications. Reminders and the daily nudge are made on your phone. Alerts about new activity come from a quiet push that iCloud sends to your phone, then your phone fetches the change and shows the notification itself. You can turn notifications off in iOS Settings.
  • To let you join with an invite code. Each code works once and expires after 7 days.

We don't sell your information, and we don't share it with advertisers or data brokers.

Who can see what you share

Everything you post or write in a group is visible to everyone in that group, by name. Think of it as speaking in a room. Please don't share other people's names, addresses, photos or messages outside the group. The person who created a group can see its reports, can remove members and can delete messages, posts and replies.

Keeping the space safe

Anyone in a group can report a post, reply or chat message from its menu, or block a person. Reported items go to the person who created the group, who can remove them. The group creator can also remove a neighbor. Foul language, abuse and hate are not allowed (see the Terms). If something is not being handled, email us and we will do what we can. See "Questions and complaints" in the Terms.

Your choices and deleting your data

  • Leave a group. In a mohalla, tap ⋯ then Leave this mohalla. In a parivar, tap ⋯ on the parivar then Leave this parivar. Leaving removes your name, answers, RSVP, claimed items, messages, posts and replies from that group.
  • Delete a group you created. The same menu says Delete. This deletes the group and everything in it for every member, and its invite code stops working. It can't be undone.
  • Remove the app. Deleting the app removes what is stored on your phone. It doesn't leave your groups, so leave them first if you want your content gone.
  • Block or hide. Blocking a person or reporting something hides it for you right away.

iCloud data is also managed in iOS Settings under your name, then iCloud. Apple's own privacy policy covers how iCloud handles it.

Children

Chai Pe Charcha is not made for children under 13, and we don't knowingly collect information from them. If you believe a child has joined a group, email us and we will help the group's creator remove the account's content.

Security

Group content is stored with Apple's iCloud and CloudKit, which encrypt data in transit and at rest. An invite code is like a key: whoever uses it first gets in, so give it only to people you know.

Changes

If this policy changes, we'll update the date at the top. If a change is significant, we'll make a reasonable effort to let you know.
